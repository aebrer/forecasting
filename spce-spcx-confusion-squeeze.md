## Core Thesis

On June 12, 2026 — the day SpaceX (SPCX) begins trading — Virgin Galactic (SPCE) will experience a short squeeze and/or retail confusion spike of **>500% from its June 4, 2026 closing price**. The stock will open elevated, spike violently in the first 1–2 hours of trading, and collapse back down by end-of-day or within 48 hours.

## Key Arguments

### Historical Precedent is Absurd
- **Tweeter Home Entertainment (TWTRQ):** Spiked **~1,500%** on Twitter's IPO day (2013) despite being a bankrupt electronics retailer. Confused retail bought the wrong ticker.
- **Signal Advance (SIGL):** Went from $0.60 to $38 (**~6,000%**) in hours when Elon Musk tweeted "Use Signal" (referring to the app, not the company).
- **Zoom Technologies (ZOOM):** Spiked on Zoom Video's (ZM) IPO as retail confused the tickers.

Ticker confusion is not a theory. It is an empirically documented retail behavior that violates every assumption of market efficiency.

### SPCE is Uniquely Dangerous as a Confusion Target
1. **Structurally similar ticker:** SPCX vs. SPCE — both 4 letters, SPC-prefix, one-letter difference.
2. **Cognitively plausible mistake:** SPCE is a real, operating space company. Unlike Tweeter (bankrupt retailer), a confused buyer sees "Virgin Galactic" and thinks "yes, space stock, this makes sense."
3. **Low nominal price:** At ~$4.83, SPCE looks "affordable" compared to SPCX at $135. Fractional-share platforms remove this barrier, but psychology still matters.
4. **Intentional proxy buying:** Retail unable to buy SPCX (no allocation, price too high, broker doesn't support) may intentionally buy SPCE as a "cheap space proxy" — behavior that mimics confusion at the aggregate level.

### Squeeze Mechanics Are Primed
- **Short interest:** ~24% of float (23M shares)
- **Borrow fee:** 11.23% and rising (was 6% two weeks ago; spiked to 25% in April)
- **Shares available to borrow:** Only ~6,000 as of June 4, 2026
- **Days to cover:** 2.48
- **Options activity:** Significant call OI at $5–6 strikes for June 18 expiry. Max pain at $4.00.

This is a powder keg. Any significant retail inflow will force dealers to delta-hedge by buying shares, which squeezes shorts, which creates reflexive buying.

### The Confused Retail Hasn't Shown Up Yet
- SPCE ran from ~$2.13 (March 30) to ~$8.90 recently, then crashed back to ~$4.83. This first leg was driven by informed front-runners, momentum algos, and space-sector ETF flows.
- The **actual confused retail** — people who hear "SpaceX is public today!" on the morning news, open their broker app, and buy the first space ticker they see — are a disjoint population. They have not bought yet. They will act on June 12.

### Media Coverage Will Be Inescapable
- SpaceX IPO is the largest in US history ($1.75T valuation, $75B raise).
- Every news outlet, TikTok finance bro, podcast, and push notification will cover it.
- The signal-to-noise ratio for "how do I buy SpaceX stock?" will be near-zero. SPCE will catch the overflow.
